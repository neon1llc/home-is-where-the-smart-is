If you're the household tech lead, you're also its single point of failure. This chapter makes sure someone else can step in.

## Legacy and recovery contacts

Each major platform lets you name someone who can access your account if you die or can't use it:

- **Apple:** Legacy Contact, and a separate Account Recovery Contact, in Sign-In & Security settings.
- **Google:** Inactive Account Manager, in the account's Data & privacy settings.
- **Facebook and Instagram:** a legacy contact or memorialization settings.
- **Password managers:** 1Password's emergency kit, Bitwarden's emergency access, and similar features.

Set these up for every adult, and help parents set theirs.

## The emergency card

Fill in the [emergency card](../templates/emergency-card.md): where the household tech sheet is, how to get into the password manager in an emergency, who to call for help, and key account providers. Keep it with your will or other important papers, and tell a second adult where it is.

## What the family will need

- Access to email, which unlocks most other accounts.
- The password manager, or its emergency access.
- Phone passcodes. A locked phone with two-step sign-in on it can block access to everything else.
- The photos and documents, and which cloud service holds them.
- A list of subscriptions and automatic payments to cancel or keep.

## Talk about it

Have the conversation once, with your partner or a trusted family member, and again whenever something big changes. It's a short, practical talk that saves weeks of trouble.

## Do this

- [ ] Emergency access set up in the password manager.

- [ ] A second adult knows where everything is.
