---
title: "3. Backups"
description: "Protect the photos, documents, and phones your family can't replace, with backups that run on their own and a restore you've tested."
---

A dropped phone or a laptop that won't start can take years of photos with it, if they existed nowhere else. Backups are the fix.

## The 3-2-1 idea

Keep **three** copies of anything you can't replace, on **two** different kinds of storage, with **one** copy away from home. For most households that's:

1. The original, on the phone or computer.
2. A cloud copy: iCloud Photos, Google Photos, OneDrive, or Dropbox.
3. A second copy you control: an external drive at home, or a second cloud service.

## What to back up

- **Phones.** Turn on iCloud Backup (iPhone) or Google One backup (Android), and photo sync. Check there's enough cloud storage, because backups stop when it's full.
- **Computers.** Time Machine on a Mac, or File History or OneDrive on Windows, to an external drive. Add a cloud backup service for an off-site copy.
- **Shared family photos.** A shared album or family plan keeps everyone's photos in one place, not spread across six phones.
- **Important documents.** Scans of IDs, insurance, property records, and medical information, in an encrypted location such as your password manager or an encrypted cloud folder.

## Test a restore

Until you've restored from a backup, you don't know it works. Once a year, pick a few photos and a document from a year ago and restore them to a different device. If it works, write down the date. If it doesn't, you've found the problem on a calm day.

## Do this

- [ ] Phone backups and photo sync on for everyone, with enough storage.
- [ ] Computer backups running to an external drive and a cloud service.
- [ ] Important documents scanned and stored encrypted.
- [ ] A restore tested, with the date written on the household tech sheet.
