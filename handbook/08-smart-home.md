title: "8. The smart home"
description: "Cameras, doorbells, speakers, TVs, and plugs: buy ones that get updates, set them up safely, and keep them off your main network."
---

Every smart device is a small computer on your network that rarely gets looked at. Most are fine. The ones that cause trouble are cheap, never updated, and still using their factory password.

## Before you buy

- Choose brands that publish how long they'll provide security updates, and support two-step sign-in on the account.
- Look for the U.S. Cyber Trust Mark on the box where it's offered, and support for Matter, a standard that makes devices work across brands.
- Skip devices that need an account with an unknown company, or an app with poor reviews about security.

## Setting up

1. Put the device on the **guest or devices network** ([chapter 4](04-router-and-wifi.md)).
2. Change any default password, and turn on two-step sign-in for the app's account.
3. Turn on automatic firmware updates.
4. Turn off features you won't use: remote access, microphones you don't need, and data sharing.

## Cameras and doorbells

- Point indoor cameras away from bedrooms and bathrooms, and turn them off or cover them when you're home if you prefer.
- Use end-to-end encryption if the camera offers it.
- Check who has access to the feeds in the app, and remove old phones and people.
- Be considerate of neighbors and visitors: avoid recording into other people's homes, and check local laws on recording audio.

## Voice assistants and smart TVs

Review the privacy settings: voice recording history, whether recordings are reviewed by people, and TV viewing data sharing (often called ACR or "viewing information"). Turn off what you don't want.

Ready, Secure, Go has the detail: [Secure your cameras and smart devices](https://github.com/neon1llc/ready-secure-go/blob/main/content/practices/devices-and-updates/dev-iot-and-cameras.md).
