title: "5. Devices and updates"
description: "Automatic updates, screen locks, encryption, and find-my-device on every phone, tablet, and computer in the house."
---

Most attacks on home devices use holes that were fixed months ago. Updates close them. The other three basics keep a lost device from becoming a lost identity.

## The four basics, on every device

1. **Automatic updates** for the operating system and apps. On phones, they're in Settings under General or System. On computers, check Windows Update or macOS Software Update, and the browser's own settings.
2. **A screen lock**: a six-digit PIN or longer, with Face ID, Touch ID, or a fingerprint for convenience.
3. **Encryption.** Modern iPhones and Android phones encrypt automatically once there's a screen lock. On computers, turn on FileVault (Mac) or BitLocker or Device Encryption (Windows), and save the recovery key in the password manager.
4. **Find my device**, with remote lock and erase: Find My on Apple devices, Find My Device on Android and Windows.

## Restarting matters

Many updates only finish after a restart. If someone's phone or laptop hasn't been restarted in weeks, it's probably behind.

## Antivirus

Windows and macOS include good built-in protection (Microsoft Defender and XProtect). Keep it on and updated. Extra paid security suites are optional; be wary of any that came preinstalled with nagging pop-ups.

## Old devices

A device that no longer gets security updates should come off the internet, or out of the house. [Chapter 10](10-buying-and-retiring.md) covers how to check and how to retire it safely.

- [ ] Automatic updates on for every device and browser.
- [ ] Screen lock and encryption on every phone, tablet, and computer.
- [ ] Find my device on, and every adult knows how to use it.
