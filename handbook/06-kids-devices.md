title: "6. Kids' devices"
description: "A plan for children's tablets and first phones: family accounts, age-appropriate controls, and the conversations that matter more than the settings."
---

Settings help, but the conversation does most of the work. A child who knows they can tell you about something weird online, without losing their phone, is safer than one behind the strictest filter.

## Set up through a family account

Create the child's account inside your family group, rather than letting them use yours or make their own:

- **Apple:** Family Sharing, then Screen Time for the child's account.
- **Google and Android:** Family Link.
- **Microsoft, Xbox, and Windows:** Microsoft Family Safety.
- **Game consoles:** Nintendo Parental Controls, PlayStation Family Management, and Xbox Family Settings.

This gives you purchase approval, age ratings, screen time limits, and location sharing, and you can loosen them as they grow.

## By age, roughly

| Age | A sensible starting point |
|---|---|
| Under 8 | Shared family tablet, used in common rooms. Apps you've chosen. No open web browser or chat. |
| 8 to 12 | Their own tablet or a basic phone. Purchase approval on. Downtime overnight. Chat only with approved contacts. Talk about what's okay to share. |
| 13 to 15 | A smartphone, with limits you agree on together and loosen as trust grows. Social media only at the platform's minimum age, with private profiles. |
| 16 and up | Most controls off. Keep two-step sign-in, find my device, and the agreement that they can always come to you. |

## The conversations

- **Nobody online who asks for secrets or photos is a friend.** If anyone does, tell a parent, and you won't be in trouble.
- **Never share sign-in codes or passwords**, even with friends. Account theft is common in gaming.
- **Money scams aimed at kids**: free in-game currency, "skins," and gift cards.
- **Sextortion**: if anyone threatens to share pictures, don't pay, stop replying, keep the messages, and tell a parent. Take It Down (takeitdown.ncmec.org) can help remove images of minors.

Keep phones charging outside bedrooms overnight, at least for younger kids. It helps sleep more than any app.

- [ ] Each child's account set up inside the family group.
- [ ] Age-appropriate controls on for every device and console.
- [ ] Two-step sign-in on any account old enough to have it.
